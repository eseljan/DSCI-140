## Overview

Data Science is the art of extracting value from data. It requires an integrated use of statistics, computer science, and scientific reasoning. This course is your gateway to data science and will give you the foundational skills to solve problems with data. By the end, you will be able to wrangle, explore, visualize, analyze, and communicate data. All analysis is taught scientifically and reproducibly using R.

No previous programming or statistical experience is assumed.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, you should be able to: \* Identify, understand, and manipulate various data types and structures \* Read in data from text, tabular formats, and structured web sources \* Understand various data structures in R (vectors, lists, data frames) \* Appropriately manipulate data in numerical, string, and factor formats \* Apply basic data wrangling techniques to prepare data for analysis \* Recognize and produce "tidy" data \* Join datasets \* Produce new variables \* Apply appropriate descriptive methods to summarize data and identify associations and relationships \* Find appropriate summary statistics \* Explore data with contingency tables (e.g., frequency tables, means by group) \* Demonstrate proficiency in data visualization \* Choose the right chart \* Utilize appropriate measurement strategies \* Follow best practices: attention to ink ratio, proper label and legend formatting, and strategic use of color, reference lines, or graphics

## Course Structure

The major components of the course are: \* **Homework** — assigned (mostly) weekly, posted Tuesday, due Friday by 5 PM on Google Classroom \* **Ongoing Cheat Sheet** — a cumulative, self-authored reference of R functions learned in the course, permitted on all exams \* **Monday Readings** - applied readings to contextualize what can go wrong in data analysis \* **Data Diary Assignment** — a self-collected 1-3 day dataset on a topic of your choosing, plus a data dictionary and written reflection on representing human experience as numbers \* **Midterm and Final Exam** — multiple choice and coding exercises, closed note/internet except your cheat sheet \* **Final Data Journalism Project** — a short (2-4 page, 5 max) original data analysis written in data-journalism style \* **Class Participation** — attendance and active participation, including arriving prepared on Mondays (see Readings below)

See the full syllabus for grading weights and late policy.

## Monday Readings

Most Mondays, we spend the first 10 minutes of class discussing a short piece of applied data science work. Readings are posted in the [course materials folder](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1KAB0hj3UfqPetbcWQxHviPkAok8rbXpK?usp=drive_link) and are short — all students are expected to read them before class and come prepared to answer:

1.  What's the headline claim?
2.  What's the data, in plain terms?
3.  What number or decision is doing the real work?
4.  What went wrong?
5.  What would "doing it right" look like?
6.  One-sentence lesson to carry forward.
